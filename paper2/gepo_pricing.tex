\begin{abstract}
We present an entropy-regularized growth-maximization framework for
ranking discrete-payoff contingent claims in incomplete markets,
indexed by an ambiguity-aversion parameter $k \ge 0$. The framework
takes the per-trade Kelly log-growth $\G(p, x) := \ln G(p, x)$ as the
growth signal, where $G$ is the Kelly growth rate (per-trade wealth
multiplier). Two equivalent forms appear:
\begin{align*}
J_k(p, x) &\;=\; \G(p, x) - k \cdot \KL(p \| u), \\
\GROUND(p, x, k) &\;=\; \exp(J_k(p, x)) \;=\; \frac{G(p, x)}{\exp(k \cdot \KL(p \| u))}.
\end{align*}
Part of our contribution is the recognition that the \emph{intrinsic
GROUND ratio} of \citet{mercurio2020} is exactly the multiplicative
(exponentiated) form of the additive functional $J_k$ of
\citet{hansen2008robustness} and \citet{maccheroni2006ambiguity}: not
an approximation, modification, or analog, but the same object in
different units. The additive form $J_k$ is convenient for analytic
manipulation; the multiplicative form $\GROUND$ admits the direct
economic reading ``per-trade wealth multiplier divided by entropic
risk multiplier'' and is the practical scalar stored per claim in
the empirical work. To our knowledge $\KL$ in this role is the first
formal risk measure proposed for discrete-payoff option strategies,
where conventional variance- or VaR-based measures are ill-defined.
We establish three results: (i) ranking by $\GROUND$ is identical
to ranking by $J_k$, by the construction $\GROUND = \exp(J_k)$;
(ii) the limiting cases recover pure Kelly growth selection
($k \to 0$) and maximum-entropy selection ($k \to \infty$); (iii)
the induced indifference-price functional reduces to the
minimum-entropy martingale measure expectation as $k \to 0$,
embedding $\GROUND$ in the incomplete-markets pricing-kernel
literature \citep{frittelli2000,follmer2002}. The criterion is
empirically validated in companion work on six years of S\&P 100
weekly credit spreads \citep{mercurio2026empirical}, where it
produces a Sharpe ratio of $1.60$ on a five-year in-sample window
and $3.25$ on a strict 70-week holdout.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The Kelly criterion \citep{kelly1956,maclean2011} prescribes the
growth-optimal sizing fraction for a repeatable bet under known
probabilities. Two well-documented difficulties arise when applying it
to derivative selection in practice. First, the probabilities are
\emph{not known}; they are estimated, typically from
implied-volatility-derived risk-neutral distributions
\citep{breeden1978} or from realised statistics, and small errors in
the probability estimates can produce large errors in the prescribed
fraction \citep{maclean2011}. Second, the Kelly objective rewards
distributions with sharply concentrated mass on a single outcome, which
in a derivatives context means rewarding implied distributions whose
sharpness reflects measurement noise as much as genuine economic
signal.

Both difficulties motivate a \emph{regularized} growth criterion: an
objective that retains the growth-maximization core but penalizes
distributional configurations whose informational sharpness is suspect.
A natural candidate for the penalty is the Kullback--Leibler divergence
of the implied distribution from a maximally-uncertain reference,
typically the uniform distribution on the discrete outcome space. This
paper formalizes that idea.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We make four contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item We formulate the entropy-regularized growth selection problem
          for discrete-payoff contingent claims in incomplete markets
          and characterize its optimal selection rule
          (Section~\ref{sec:framework}).
    \item We prove that the optimal selection rule coincides with
          ranking by the \emph{GROUND ratio} $\GROUND = G \, b^{-k\,\KL}$
          (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, Section~\ref{sec:main}).
    \item We characterize the limiting behaviour: $k \to 0$ recovers
          pure Kelly growth selection, $k \to \infty$ recovers
          maximum-entropy (uniform-closest) selection, and the
          intermediate regime interpolates monotonically
          (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).
    \item We connect the induced reservation-price functional to the
          minimum-entropy martingale measure (MEMM) of
          \citet{frittelli2000}: in the unregularized limit $k \to 0$,
          the GROUND reservation price coincides with the MEMM
          expectation of the claim payoff, placing GROUND in the
          incomplete-markets pricing-kernel lineage
          (Section~\ref{sec:pricing}).
    \item We state the framework in natural-log units in a form that
          subsumes the binary-option, discrete-state, and
          continuous-state cases without modification
          (Section~\ref{sec:continuous}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.}
The use of information-theoretic quantities as risk measures in
portfolio optimization was first proposed by
\citet{mercurio2020portfolio}, who argued that entropy provides a
natural alternative to variance when the return distribution is
discrete or otherwise ill-suited to mean-variance treatment. The
specialization to $\KL$ from a uniform reference as a measure of risk
for discrete-payoff option strategies followed in
\citet{mercurio2020binary}, where we established $\KL$ as a convex
risk measure in the sense of \citet{follmer2002} on the two-state
binary-wager simplex, with the uniform distribution playing the role
of the minimum-risk reference. The present paper
extends that construction in two directions: from two states to a
general $n$-state outcome space (with $n = 3$ corresponding to the
vertical credit spread in the companion empirical work), and from a
single-claim risk measure to a return-over-risk ratio in which $\KL$
appears as the denominator of an entropy-regularized growth
objective.

The GROUND ratio itself was first introduced in
\citet{mercurio2020} as a portfolio-selection criterion for option
strategies under a generalized entropic framework. The original form
is \emph{comparative}: a candidate $a$ is scored against a per-period
reference candidate $b$ (typically the minimum-divergence member of
the pool) by
\[
\Gamma_{a|b} \;=\; \frac{\G(p_a, x_a) - \G(p_b, x_b)}{\KL(p_a \| u) - \KL(p_b \| u)},
\qquad \G := \ln G.
\]
The comparative form ranks the pool relative to a per-period
reference candidate $b$ (taken as the minimum-divergence member of
the pool). The denominator vanishes when the candidate being scored
coincides with the reference, so ties at minimum-$\KL$ are handled
procedurally --- $b$ is excluded from scoring, and any further ties
at $\KL_b$ are broken by an external rule. This cross-candidate
coupling is awkward in a streaming weekly-selection setting,
where the menu changes week-to-week and a per-week reference must be
recomputed.

The present paper introduces the \emph{intrinsic} GROUND ratio: a
per-candidate score that requires no reference,
\[
\GROUND(a, k) \;=\; \frac{\exp(\G(p_a, x_a))}{\exp\!\bigl(k \cdot \KL(p_a \| u)\bigr)}
              \;=\; \frac{G(p_a, x_a)}{\exp\!\bigl(k \cdot \KL(p_a \| u)\bigr)}
              \;=\; \exp\!\bigl(\G(p_a, x_a) - k \cdot \KL(p_a \| u)\bigr)
              \;=\; \exp(J_k(a)).
\]
The chain of equalities makes the construction explicit: the
intrinsic GROUND ratio is the exponentiated form of the additive
Hansen-Sargent functional $J_k$. The companion empirical paper
\citep{mercurio2026empirical} reports validation of the intrinsic
form on six years of S\&P 100 weekly credit spreads.

\paragraph{Relation to entropy-regularized utility.}
The additive form $J_k = \G - k \cdot \KL$ that arises by taking the
log of the intrinsic GROUND ratio coincides exactly with the
multiplier-preferences functional of
\citet{hansen2008robustness} and the variational preferences of
\citet{maccheroni2006ambiguity}, with $\G = \ln G$ playing the role
of the growth signal --- the natural per-trade signal for Kelly
wagers. Our setting differs from the multiplier-preferences
literature in that the agent does not optimize over $p$ but
evaluates an exogenously-given menu of $\{p_i\}$, so the appropriate
object is the multiplicative cross-candidate ratio $\GROUND$ rather
than the inner-sup-over-$p$ value functional of
\citet{hansen2008robustness}.

\section{Framework}\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Probability space and outcome structure}

Let $(\Omega, \F, \PP)$ be a probability space and let
$\X = \{\omega_1, \ldots, \omega_n\}$ be a finite outcome set with
$|\X| = n \ge 2$. We work in the single-period setting throughout the
main results; the multi-period extension is discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:open}.

\begin{definition}[Contingent claim]\label{def:claim}
A \emph{contingent claim} on $\X$ is a triple
$\C = (x, p, c)$ where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $x: \X \to \R$ is the per-unit-stake payoff vector,
          normalized so that $\min_{\omega \in \X} x(\omega) = -1$ and
          $\max_{\omega \in \X} x(\omega) = +1$;
    \item $p$ is a probability vector on $\X$, i.e.\ $p(\omega) \ge 0$
          for all $\omega$ and $\sum_\omega p(\omega) = 1$;
    \item $c \ge 0$ is the per-unit-stake cost (price) of entering the
          claim.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Normalization]\label{rem:normalize}
The normalization $\min x = -1, \max x = +1$ is without loss of
generality for the selection problem: a credit spread with credit $C$
and max-loss $M$ has gross-payoff outcomes $\{+C, +\alpha C, -M\}$ which
under the per-dollar-of-risk normalization $w \mapsto w/M$ become
$\{C/M, \alpha C/M, -1\}$, and a further scale-rebasing
$\tilde x(\omega) = x(\omega) / M$ produces payoffs in $[-1, +1]$. The
empirical work in \citet{mercurio2026empirical} normalizes outcomes to
$\{+1, +\alpha, -1\}$ with $\alpha \in [-1, +1]$, which is the
specialization studied in the canonical configuration.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Growth and divergence functionals}

For a fixed claim $\C = (x, p, c)$ and a wager fraction $w \in [0, 1)$
of the agent's bankroll, the post-bet bankroll multiplier is
$1 + w \, x(\omega)$ in state $\omega$. The Kelly log-growth at wager
$w$ is
\begin{equation}
\ln G(w; p, x) = \sum_{\omega \in \X} p(\omega) \log\bigl(1 + w \, x(\omega)\bigr).
\label{eq:growth}
\end{equation}
We assume throughout that the wager is admissible, i.e.\
$1 + w \, x(\omega) > 0$ for all $\omega$, which is satisfied for
$w \in [0, 1)$ when $x \ge -1$.

The Kelly-optimal fraction is
\begin{equation}
w^\star(p, x) = \arg\max_{w \in [0, 1)} \ln G(w; p, x),
\label{eq:wstar}
\end{equation}
which exists and is unique under standard non-degeneracy on $p$ (see
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, Lemma~\ref{lem:wstar}). We define the
\emph{Kelly growth rate} as the per-trade wealth multiplier at the
optimizer:
\begin{equation}
G(p, x) := \exp\!\bigl(\ln G(w^\star(p, x); p, x)\bigr)
        \;=\; \prod_{\omega} \bigl(1 + w^\star x(\omega)\bigr)^{p(\omega)}.
\label{eq:G}
\end{equation}
$G(p, x) \ge 1$ for growth-positive claims (the geometric mean of
positive bankroll multipliers exceeds 1 iff log-growth is positive).
In the sequel $G$ always refers to the growth rate; the underlying
log-growth signal is denoted $\G := \ln G$ (lowercase, to
disambiguate from the multiplier $G$). Both quantities are
candidate-level functions of $(p, x)$, related by $G = \exp(\G)$.

The relative entropy of $p$ from the uniform distribution
$u_n(\omega) = 1/n$ is
\begin{equation}
\KL(p \| u_n) = \sum_{\omega \in \X} p(\omega) \log\!\left(
    \frac{p(\omega)}{1/n}\right) = \log n - H(p),
\label{eq:KL}
\end{equation}
where $H(p) = -\sum_\omega p(\omega) \log p(\omega)$ is the Shannon
entropy. We work throughout in natural-log units (nats), so that
$\KL(p \| u_n) \in [0, \ln n]$ with the upper bound attained when $p$
is a Dirac mass; for the three-outcome credit-spread case
$\ln n = \ln 3 \approx 1.099$.

\begin{remark}[Choice of log base]\label{rem:logbase}
We adopt natural log throughout, the canonical convention for both
the Kelly log-utility and the information-theoretic divergence. This
makes the framework state-space agnostic: the same formulas apply
unchanged to binary, $n$-state discrete, and continuous-outcome
settings. The empirical work uses natural log as well, with
$\KL(p \| u_3) \in [0, \ln 3]$ in the three-state credit-spread
setting. Any other log base produces an algebraically equivalent
ranker at a re-scaled $k$, so the choice of base is purely a unit
convention.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Menu of claims}

\begin{definition}[Claim menu]\label{def:menu}
A \emph{claim menu} is a finite collection
$\mathcal{M} = \{\C_1, \ldots, \C_M\}$ of contingent claims on a common
outcome space $\X$. The agent must select exactly one claim from
$\mathcal{M}$ (or none). Selection is myopic in this paper: the agent
does not anticipate future menus.
\end{definition}

In the empirical setting, $\mathcal{M}$ is the Greek-filtered
candidate pool of credit spreads at a given weekly entry, with
$|\mathcal{M}|$ ranging from a few dozen to several hundred. The
restriction to single-period myopic selection is a limitation; the
multi-period treatment requires dynamic-programming machinery beyond
the scope of this paper.


\section{Main result}\label{sec:main}

\subsection{The additive objective $J_k$}

For a contingent claim $\C = (x, p, c)$, let
$\G(p, x) := \ln G(p, x)$ denote the Kelly log-growth signal, with
$G$ the per-trade Kelly growth rate from Equation~\eqref{eq:G}. We
take as our primary functional the additive
entropy-regularized expected-utility objective of
\citet{hansen2008robustness} and \citet{maccheroni2006ambiguity}:
\begin{equation}
J_k(p, x) \;:=\; \G(p, x) - k \cdot \KL(p \| u_n).
\label{eq:Jk}
\end{equation}
This is the form we use for all analytic manipulation throughout the
paper --- derivatives, limits, the Donsker-Varadhan correspondence
of Section~\ref{sec:pricing} --- and it is the form a reader of the
multiplier-preferences literature will immediately recognise.

\subsection{The intrinsic GROUND ratio $\GROUND(\cdot, k) := \exp(J_k)$}

Exponentiating $J_k$ yields the same construction expressed
multiplicatively. We call this the \emph{intrinsic GROUND ratio} and
denote it $\GROUND$, with the ambiguity-aversion multiplier $k$
carried as a parameter:
\begin{equation}
\GROUND(p, x, k) \;:=\; \exp\!\bigl(J_k(p, x)\bigr)
                  \;=\; \frac{\exp(\G(p, x))}{\exp\!\bigl(k \cdot \KL(p \| u_n)\bigr)}
                  \;=\; \frac{G(p, x)}{\exp\!\bigl(k \cdot \KL(p \| u_n)\bigr)}.
\label{eq:ground}
\end{equation}
The chain of equalities makes the construction explicit: the
multiplicative form is the exponentiation of the additive form, and
since $\exp(\G) = G$ by definition, the numerator collapses to the
per-trade Kelly growth rate $G$. The denominator $\exp(k \KL)$ is an
entropic risk multiplier. The ratio is dimensionless and reads
``per-trade wealth multiplier divided by entropic risk multiplier.''
Both numerator and denominator are $\ge 1$ for growth-positive
claims with $\KL \ge 0$, so $\GROUND(p, x, k) \in (0, \infty)$, with
$\GROUND(p, x, k) = 1$ exactly when $J_k = 0$ (growth signal balances
divergence penalty).

The subscript $\mathrm{i}$ stands for ``intrinsic'' (a per-candidate
score requiring no reference), distinguishing it from the original
\emph{comparative} GROUND ratio of \citet{mercurio2020}, which scored
a candidate $a$ against a per-period reference $b$ via
$\Gamma_{a|b} = (\G_a - \G_b) / (\KL(p_a\|u) - \KL(p_b\|u))$. The
intrinsic $\GROUND$ is the per-candidate evolution of that
construction and the form used in the empirical work.

\paragraph{Use convention.} We work with $J_k$ analytically throughout
this paper for simplicity and consistency with the
multiplier-preferences lineage. The exponentiated $\GROUND$ form
appears in display contexts (empirical tables, the live tracker, the
weekly reports of \citet{mercurio2026empirical}) where the positive
multiplicative-ratio reading is more legible than a negative
additive log-utility number. The two are the same object: $\GROUND$
is a name we give to $\exp(J_k)$, not a separate functional.

\begin{definition}[Intrinsic GROUND ratio]\label{def:ground}
For a contingent claim $\C = (x, p, c)$, the intrinsic GROUND ratio
at parameter $k \ge 0$ is defined by
\eqref{eq:ground}, equivalently by $\GROUND = \exp(J_k)$ with
$J_k$ from \eqref{eq:Jk}.
\end{definition}

The two forms are not approximations of one another: they are the
same construction expressed additively in log-space and
multiplicatively in growth-space. Selection by argmax of
\eqref{eq:Jk} and argmax of \eqref{eq:ground} are trivially
equivalent via the monotone log.

We assume $G(p, x) > 0$ throughout the main result; the case
$G \le 0$ corresponds to candidates the agent should not enter at any
size and is discussed in Remark~\ref{rem:Gneg}.

\subsection{Selection equivalence}

The additive form $J_k$ in \eqref{eq:Jk} and the multiplicative
form $\GROUND$ in \eqref{eq:ground} produce the same selection on
any menu of growth-positive claims, by construction.

\begin{theorem}[Selection equivalence]\label{thm:main}
Fix $k \ge 0$ and let $\mathcal{M} = \{\C_1, \ldots, \C_M\}$ be a menu
of claims with $G(p_i, x_i) > 0$ for every $i$. The following two
selection rules produce the same optimal claim:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Additive (Hansen-Sargent): $i^\star = \arg\max_{i} J_k(p_i, x_i)
        = \arg\max_i \bigl[\G(p_i, x_i) - k \KL(p_i \| u_n)\bigr]$.
    \item Multiplicative (intrinsic GROUND): $i^\star = \arg\max_{i} \GROUND(p_i, x_i, k)
        = \arg\max_i \bigl[ G(p_i, x_i) / \exp(k \KL(p_i \| u_n))\bigr]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:ground}, $\GROUND = \exp(J_k)$. The
exponential is a strictly increasing transformation, so
$\arg\max_i \GROUND(p_i, x_i, k) = \arg\max_i J_k(p_i, x_i)$. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[$G \le 0$ case]\label{rem:Gneg}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} requires $G(p_i, x_i) > 0$ on the menu, which
is the empirically relevant case (only growth-positive candidates are
considered for entry; the canonical configuration filters
$\G(p_i, x_i) > 0$ at the candidate-construction stage). When some
candidates have $\G \le 0$ (negative-expected-log-growth trades),
$J_k = \G - k\KL$ is non-monotone in $\KL$ and the additive form
requires regularization.
\end{remark}


\section{Limiting cases}\label{sec:limits}

The two limits $k \to 0$ and $k \to \infty$ characterize the boundary
behaviour of the regularization. Both are economically meaningful and
recover well-studied selection rules.

\begin{proposition}[Kelly limit]\label{prop:kelly-limit}
Fix a menu $\mathcal{M}$ with $G(p_i, x_i) > 0$ for all $i$. As
$k \to 0^+$, the GROUND-optimal selection $i^\star_k = \arg\max_i
\GROUND(p_i, x_i, k)$ converges to the pure-Kelly-optimal selection
\begin{equation*}
i^\star_0 = \arg\max_i G(p_i, x_i).
\end{equation*}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Continuity: $\GROUND(p, x, k) = G(p, x) \cdot \exp(-k \KL(p\|u_n) \log n)
\to G(p, x)$ as $k \to 0$, uniformly in $(p, x)$ on a finite menu.
The argmax of a uniformly convergent sequence on a finite set
converges to the argmax of the limit, modulo ties (resolved by
arbitrary rule).
\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Maximum-entropy limit]\label{prop:maxent-limit}
Fix a menu $\mathcal{M}$ with $G(p_i, x_i) > 0$ for all $i$. As
$k \to \infty$, the GROUND-optimal selection $i^\star_k$ converges to
the minimum-divergence selection
\begin{equation*}
i^\star_\infty = \arg\min_i \KL(p_i \| u_n),
\end{equation*}
i.e., the claim whose implied distribution is closest to uniform.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take logs: $\log_n \GROUND(p, x, k) = \log_n G(p, x) - k \KL(p \| u_n)$.
For large $k$, the term $-k \KL$ dominates $\log_n G$ (which is
bounded on the finite menu), so $\arg\max_i \log_n \GROUND(p_i, x_i, k)
\to \arg\min_i \KL(p_i \| u_n)$ as $k \to \infty$. Strict monotonicity
of $\log_n$ preserves the argmax. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Interpolation]
\label{rem:interpolation}
The two limits bracket a continuous family of selection rules indexed
by $k$. At $k = 0$, the agent ignores distributional concentration and
picks pure growth. At $k = \infty$, the agent ignores growth and picks
the most uniform (least informative) implied distribution. For
intermediate $k$, the agent trades off the two. The empirical work
\citep{mercurio2026empirical} reports a $k$-sweep on the in-sample
window over $k \in \{10, 15, 20, 25, 30\}$ in natural-log units (nats)
and finds a Sharpe plateau at $k \in [20, 30]$. The chosen canonical
$k = 20$ sits at the low edge of the plateau and was frozen before
any out-of-sample data was scored.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Monotonicity in $k$ on a fixed claim]
\label{cor:monotone-k}
For any fixed claim with $\KL(p \| u_n) > 0$,
$\GROUND(p, x, k)$ is strictly decreasing in $k$. For any claim with
$\KL(p \| u_n) = 0$ (i.e.\ $p = u_n$), $\GROUND(p, x, k) = G(p, x)$ is
constant in $k$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\partial_k \GROUND = -\GROUND \cdot \KL(p \| u_n) \log n$, which
is strictly negative when $\KL(p \| u_n) > 0$ and zero otherwise.
\qed
\end{proof}


\section{Connection to incomplete-markets pricing}\label{sec:pricing}

The selection result of Section~\ref{sec:main} characterizes which
claim from a menu the agent should buy. We now consider the dual
question: what is the agent's reservation price for a single claim?
This is the standard utility-indifference pricing question
\citep{rouge2000,delbaen2002}, specialized to the entropy-regularized
log-growth objective.

\subsection{Reservation price under regularized growth}

\begin{definition}[$k$-regularized reservation price]\label{def:reservation}
Fix $k \ge 0$ and a contingent claim $\C = (x, p, c)$ with
$G(p, x) > 0$. The \emph{$k$-regularized reservation price} of $\C$ is
the maximum cost $c^\star_k$ at which the agent's regularized
growth from entering $\C$ at the Kelly-optimal fraction equals the
no-trade baseline:
\begin{equation}
c^\star_k(p, x) = \arg\max_{c} \Bigl\{ J_k(p, x) - c \cdot w^\star(p, x)
    \ge 0 \Bigr\}.
\label{eq:reservation}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

This definition specializes the standard utility-indifference price to
the regularized log-growth utility, with the cost-of-entry term
deducted linearly in the wager fraction $w^\star$. The economic
interpretation is the same as the standard one: $c^\star_k$ is the
highest price at which the agent is willing to take the bet at its
Kelly-optimal size.

\begin{proposition}[Closed form for reservation price]\label{prop:reservation}
Under the conditions of Definition~\ref{def:reservation},
\begin{equation}
c^\star_k(p, x) = \frac{G(p, x) - k \cdot \KL(p \| u_n)}{w^\star(p, x)}
                = \frac{\log_n \GROUND(p, x, k)}{w^\star(p, x)}.
\label{eq:reservation-form}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct from \eqref{eq:Jk} and \eqref{eq:reservation}, solving for $c$
at equality. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The denominator $w^\star$ is the Kelly fraction at which the bet is
sized; it converts a per-unit-bankroll growth quantity into a
per-unit-stake price. In the unregularized case $k = 0$, this is the
classical exponential-utility-indifference price specialized to log
utility, which is the well-studied reservation price under
expected-log-utility \citep{maclean2011}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Limit \texorpdfstring{$k \to 0$}{k to 0}: connection to MEMM}

We now place our pricing functional within the incomplete-markets
literature by examining what happens as the entropy penalty vanishes.
The natural reference point is the minimum-entropy martingale measure
(MEMM) of \citet{frittelli2000}, which we briefly recall.

In an incomplete market there are typically infinitely many
equivalent martingale measures $\Q$ that price a given claim
arbitrage-free. The MEMM, denoted $\Q^*$, is the unique such measure
that minimizes the relative entropy $\KL(\Q \| \PP)$ against an
agent's reference $\PP$. It plays a central role in the literature on
utility-based pricing: the marginal utility-indifference price under
exponential utility is known to converge to the MEMM expectation
$\E_{\Q^*}[\C]$ as the agent's risk aversion tends to zero
\citep{frittelli2000,delbaen2002,fujiwara2003}. The next theorem
shows that the GROUND-induced reservation price inherits the same
property in its unregularized limit.

\begin{theorem}[MEMM connection at $k = 0$]\label{thm:memm}
Let $\PP$ be the agent's reference probability measure (taken as the
uniform $u_n$ in the canonical setting). Let $\Q^*$ denote the
minimum-entropy martingale measure relative to $\PP$ on the discrete
outcome space $\X$. Then for any contingent claim $\C = (x, p, c)$
with $p$ derived from $\Q^*$ (i.e., $p = \Q^*$),
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{k \to 0^+} c^\star_k(p, x) = \E_{\Q^*}[x].
\end{equation*}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We work entirely on the finite simplex $\Delta_n$, avoiding the
measure-theoretic machinery required in the general continuous-time
setting of \citet{frittelli2000}. Without loss of generality, take
$\PP = u_n$.

\emph{Step 1: characterize $\Q^*$ by KKT.} The MEMM $\Q^*$ minimizes
$\KL(\Q \| u_n) = \sum_j \Q_j \log_n(n \Q_j)$ subject to the pricing
constraint $\sum_j \Q_j x_j = c$ and normalization $\sum_j \Q_j = 1$,
plus non-negativity $\Q_j \ge 0$ which is non-binding when $u_n$ has
full support. The Lagrangian (suppressing the inequality multipliers,
inactive at the optimum) is
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{L}(\Q, \lambda, \mu) = \sum_j \Q_j \log_n(n \Q_j)
   - \lambda \Bigl( \sum_j \Q_j x_j - c \Bigr)
   - \mu \Bigl( \sum_j \Q_j - 1 \Bigr).
\end{equation*}
Stationarity $\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial \Q_j = 0$ gives
$\log_n(n \Q_j) + 1/\ln(n) - \lambda x_j - \mu = 0$, i.e.\
$\Q_j \propto u_n(j) \, \exp(\lambda^\star \ln(n) \, x_j)$.
Normalizing,
\begin{equation}
\Q^*_j = \frac{u_n(j) \, n^{\lambda^\star x_j}}{Z(\lambda^\star)},
\qquad
Z(\lambda) = \sum_j u_n(j) \, n^{\lambda x_j},
\label{eq:memm-exp-tilt}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda^\star$ is the unique multiplier solving the pricing
constraint $\sum_j \Q^*_j x_j = c$. Existence and uniqueness of
$\lambda^\star$ follow from strict convexity of $Z(\lambda)$ when
$\mathrm{Var}_{u_n}(x) > 0$, and the absence of arbitrage ensures the
constraint is feasible.

\emph{Step 2: take the $k \to 0$ limit of $c^\star_k$.} From
Proposition~\ref{prop:reservation},
\begin{equation*}
c^\star_k(p, x) = \frac{G(p, x) - k \, \KL(p \| u_n)}{w^\star(p, x)}.
\end{equation*}
As $k \to 0$, the $-k \KL$ term vanishes and $c^\star_k \to G(p,x)/w^\star$.
The first-order condition for $w^\star$ at $w \to 0$ is
$\partial G/\partial w \big|_{w=0} = \E_p[x] \cdot (\ln n)^{-1} = 0$, so
the small-$w^\star$ regime corresponds to $\E_p[x] \to 0$. Taylor
expanding $G$ around $w = 0$:
\begin{equation*}
G(p, x) = w \, \frac{\E_p[x]}{\ln n}
        - \frac{w^2}{2} \frac{\E_p[x^2]}{\ln n} + O(w^3),
\end{equation*}
so $G(p,x) / w^\star \to \E_p[x] / \ln n$ as $\E_p[x] \to 0$ (the
denominator $w^\star$ scales linearly with $\E_p[x]$ from the FOC, so
the ratio is finite and equals $\E_p[x]$ up to the log-base unit
conversion). In log-base-$n$ units consistent with $c^\star_k$,
$\lim_{k \to 0^+} c^\star_k(p, x) = \E_p[x]$.

\emph{Step 3: specialize $p = \Q^*$.} Substituting $p = \Q^*$ from
\eqref{eq:memm-exp-tilt},
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{k \to 0^+} c^\star_k(\Q^*, x) = \E_{\Q^*}[x],
\end{equation*}
which is the MEMM expectation. Dominated convergence on the finite
simplex is automatic ($|G|$ is bounded on the compact set $\Delta_n
\times [0, 1)$), so the limit and the expectation interchange. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:memm} establishes that the GROUND-induced pricing
functional embeds the MEMM expectation as the unregularized limit. For
$k > 0$, $\GROUND$ produces a pricing functional that is
\emph{regularized} relative to MEMM in the direction of the uniform
reference. This is consistent with the empirical-measurement-error
motivation of Section~\ref{sec:intro}: when $p$ is estimated from
implied volatilities and the resulting $\Q^*$ is suspect, $k > 0$
moves the implied price toward the uniform-prior expected payoff,
trading off precision for robustness.
\end{remark}
\section{The canonical GROUND ratio}\label{sec:continuous}

We are now in a position to state what we propose as the canonical
form of the GROUND ratio: the single equation that subsumes the
binary-option case of \citet{mercurio2020binary}, the three-state
credit-spread case of the companion empirical paper
\citep{mercurio2026empirical}, and the general $n$-state discrete
case of Section~\ref{sec:main}, and that extends without modification
to any continuous-payoff derivative.

Let $\X$ be any measurable outcome space --- finite, countable, or
continuous. Let $u$ denote a reference probability distribution on
$\X$: the uniform measure when $\X$ is finite, a chosen reference
density (e.g.\ a benchmark risk-neutral density) when $\X$ is
continuous. Let $p$ denote the implied distribution of a candidate
claim with payoff function $x: \X \to \R$. Working throughout in
natural-logarithm units, we define the \emph{Kelly log-growth
functional}
\begin{equation}
G(p, x) \;=\; \sup_{w \in [0, 1)} \E_p\!\bigl[\ln(1 + w \, x)\bigr],
\label{eq:G-canonical}
\end{equation}
and the \emph{relative entropy from the reference}
\begin{equation}
\KL(p \| u) \;=\; \E_p\!\biggl[\ln \frac{p}{u}\biggr],
\label{eq:KL-canonical}
\end{equation}
where the expectation reduces to a sum in the discrete case and an
integral in the continuous case. The canonical GROUND ratio is the
following one-line object, which we propose as the central formula
of this paper:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\Gamma(p, x) \;=\; G(p, x) \cdot \exp\!\bigl( -k \, \KL(p \| u) \bigr)
\;}
\label{eq:ground-canonical}
\end{equation}
The economic content of \eqref{eq:ground-canonical} reads cleanly
once one separates the two factors. The leading factor $G(p, x)$ is
the trade's \emph{expected return}, taken at the Kelly-optimal
sizing fraction. The multiplicative factor $\exp(-k \KL(p \| u))$ is
a \emph{risk discount}: it shrinks the score in proportion to how
far the implied distribution $p$ sits from the maximum-uncertainty
reference $u$, with the elasticity controlled by the single parameter
$k$. A trade whose implied distribution is uniform (the
maximally-uninformative baseline) receives no discount; a trade
whose distribution is sharply concentrated on one outcome receives
exponentially heavy attenuation. In one sentence: the GROUND ratio
is \emph{return} $\times$ \emph{entropy-based risk discount}.

\paragraph{Why natural log.} The use of $\ln$ and $\exp$ in
\eqref{eq:ground-canonical} is the natural-units presentation. In a
continuous state space there is no integer cardinality to anchor a
``natural'' base, so natural log is the appropriate convention.
Discrete instances of \eqref{eq:ground-canonical} are obtained
simply by reading $G$ and $\KL$ in matched base-$n$ units and
writing the risk factor as $n^{-k \KL}$ in place of $\exp(-k \KL)$;
the amplification parameter $k$ is dimensionless and carries
through unchanged.

\paragraph{Three forms in one identity.} We can summarize the
evolution of the GROUND ratio through the three settings in which it
has been studied:
\begin{align*}
\text{\citet{mercurio2020binary}, binary options:} \qquad
&\Gamma \;=\; G \cdot 2^{-k \KL(p \| u_2)}, \\[2pt]
\text{Companion paper, credit spreads:} \qquad
&\Gamma \;=\; G \cdot 3^{-k \KL(p \| u_3)}, \\[2pt]
\text{General, any outcome space:} \qquad
&\Gamma \;=\; G \cdot \exp\!\bigl( -k \, \KL(p \| u) \bigr).
\end{align*}
Each line is the same equation written in the base matched to its
outcome space. The third line is the form we recommend for any
future application of the framework --- to derivatives of arbitrary
payoff structure, to sports-betting markets, or to any other setting
in which a return-over-information-risk score is required.

\paragraph{Optimal sizing.} The Kelly-optimal fraction $w^\star$
in \eqref{eq:G-canonical} satisfies the first-order condition
\begin{equation}
\E_p\!\biggl[\frac{x}{1 + w^\star \, x}\biggr] \;=\; 0,
\label{eq:cts-foc}
\end{equation}
which in the continuous case is the integral equation
$\int p(s) x(s) / (1 + w^\star x(s)) \, ds = 0$ and in the discrete
case reduces to the closed-form quadratic derived in the companion
empirical paper. The statement and proof are below.

\begin{proposition}[FOC for Kelly-optimal sizing]\label{prop:cts-foc}
Let $p$ admit a density with respect to $u$, with
$x \in L^\infty(p)$ and $x > -1$ $p$-almost everywhere. The Gâteaux
derivative of $G(w; p, x) = \E_p[\ln(1 + w x)]$ in $w$ vanishes at
$w^\star \in (0, 1)$ if and only if
$\E_p[\,x / (1 + w^\star x)\,] = 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under the integrability conditions, the dominated convergence
theorem permits differentiation under the expectation:
\begin{equation*}
\partial_w \E_p[\ln(1 + w x)]
   \;=\; \E_p\!\biggl[\frac{x}{1 + w x}\biggr].
\end{equation*}
The dominating function $p \cdot |x| / (1 - w \|x\|_\infty)$ is
$p$-integrable on the bounded support of $x$. Setting the derivative
to zero at $w^\star$ gives the stated condition.
\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why this is the right form]\label{rem:why-canonical}
A reader may ask why \eqref{eq:ground-canonical} should be regarded
as the canonical statement rather than one of its equivalent
base-$n$ specializations. Three reasons.
\emph{(i)} It is the only form under which neither $G$ nor $\KL$
carries an extraneous $\ln n$ factor; the equation reads cleanly in
the same units in which $\ln$ and $\exp$ are conventionally written.
\emph{(ii)} It does not require the outcome space to be finite, and
so applies to any derivative whose payoff is a measurable function on
a probability space --- a feature the discrete formulations cannot
offer.
\emph{(iii)} Its multiplicative structure $\Gamma = G \cdot D$ with
$D = \exp(-k \KL) \in (0, 1]$ separates the two roles of return and
risk into a product of independently interpretable factors. The
risk-discount factor $D$ is itself a natural rescaling: $D = 1$ when
$p = u$ (no risk premium), and $D \to 0$ as $\KL \to \infty$ (full
attenuation as the implied distribution becomes a Dirac mass). We
believe \eqref{eq:ground-canonical} is the natural-units statement
that future applications of the framework should invoke.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Limiting cases in the canonical form]
\label{rem:limits-canonical}
The two limits of Section~\ref{sec:limits} have especially clean
expressions under \eqref{eq:ground-canonical}. As $k \to 0$ the risk
discount $\exp(-k \KL) \to 1$ and the ratio collapses to pure Kelly
growth:
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{k \to 0^+} \Gamma(p, x) \;=\; G(p, x).
\end{equation*}
As $k \to \infty$ the discount factor drives each candidate's score
to zero at a rate set by its own $\KL$, so selection collapses to
the minimum-divergence claim:
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{k \to \infty} \arg\max_i \Gamma(p_i, x_i)
   \;=\; \arg\min_i \KL(p_i \| u).
\end{equation*}
The canonical form makes these limits read as statements about a
return-times-discount product, which is what they fundamentally are.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Structural parity with the discrete case]
The selection-equivalence theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}) and the
limiting cases of Section~\ref{sec:limits} extend to the continuous
setting without modification once \eqref{eq:ground-canonical}
replaces the discrete formula and $\int$ replaces $\sum$. What does
\emph{not} trivially extend is the closed-form quadratic for
$w^\star$: for a general continuous payoff, \eqref{eq:cts-foc} is
solved numerically. The GROUND ranking score, by contrast, remains
a single scalar in every setting.
\end{remark}

\section{Open problems and extensions}\label{sec:open}

We list directions in which the framework of this paper could be
extended. None of the following are within scope of the present paper.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Multi-period extension.}
        The single-period myopic selection in Section~\ref{sec:framework}
        is a strong simplification. The dynamic-programming extension
        treats $\GROUND$ as the per-period reward in a stochastic
        control problem and asks for the value function and the
        optimal weekly policy. The empirical work uses a weekly
        single-period treatment, so the simplification is faithful to
        practice; theoretically, however, a multi-period treatment
        would establish optimality in a stronger sense.
    \item \emph{Continuous-state limit: integrability conditions.}
        Section~\ref{sec:continuous} establishes that the
        regularized-growth selection rule passes through to a
        continuous-state setting with the differential KL divergence
        replacing the discrete one, and that the FOC for $w^\star$
        becomes an integral equation. The remaining open question is
        the precise regularity required for the FOC to admit a unique
        interior solution: bounded payoff and a density $p$ absolutely
        continuous with respect to the reference $u$ are clearly
        sufficient, but tight conditions on unbounded payoffs (long
        straddles, naked tail wings) remain to be characterised.
    \item \emph{Choice of reference measure.}
        We use the uniform reference $u_n$ throughout, which is the
        maximum-entropy measure on a finite outcome space. Alternative
        references are economically defensible: for example, the
        risk-neutral measure derived from the most-liquid at-the-money
        contract on the same underlying could play the role of $u$,
        producing a relative-information criterion rather than an
        absolute-information one. This is closer to the comparative
        form of \citet{mercurio2020} and would re-introduce
        cross-claim coupling at the price of removing the
        scale-anchored interpretation of $\KL \in [0, 1]$.
    \item \emph{Robustness to misspecification of $u$.}
        If the true reference measure differs from $u_n$ by a
        small perturbation, how does the GROUND-optimal selection
        change? This is a stability/sensitivity question with a
        natural answer in terms of the implicit-function theorem
        applied to the argmax of $J_k$. We expect the selection to be
        Lipschitz-stable in the reference measure under
        non-degenerate conditions on the menu.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Deferred proofs}\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Existence and uniqueness of \texorpdfstring{$w^\star$}{w-star}}

\begin{lemma}[Properties of $w^\star$]\label{lem:wstar}
Let $\C = (x, p, c)$ be a contingent claim with
$\min_\omega x(\omega) = -1$, $\max_\omega x(\omega) = +1$, and
$p(\omega) > 0$ for all $\omega$ such that $x(\omega) \neq 0$. The
expected log-growth $G(\cdot; p, x): [0, 1) \to \R$ defined in
\eqref{eq:growth} is strictly concave. Moreover, $G$ admits a unique
maximizer $w^\star \in [0, 1)$ if and only if
$\E_p[x] > 0$, in which case $w^\star \in (0, 1)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Strict concavity.} The function $w \mapsto \log(1 + w x(\omega))$
is concave for any $x(\omega) > -1$, and strictly concave when
$x(\omega) \neq 0$. The sum $G$ is therefore concave in $w$ on
$[0, 1)$, and strictly concave provided the support of $p$ contains at
least two outcomes with distinct payoffs (which is the non-degenerate
case).

\emph{Existence and characterization of $w^\star$.} The first-order
condition
\begin{equation*}
G'(w; p, x) = \sum_\omega \frac{p(\omega) \, x(\omega)}{1 + w \, x(\omega)} = 0
\end{equation*}
must have a solution in $(0, 1)$ iff $G'(0; p, x) = \E_p[x] > 0$ and
$G'(w; p, x) \to -\infty$ as $w \to 1^-$ (which holds whenever
$p(\omega) > 0$ for some $\omega$ with $x(\omega) = -1$). Strict
concavity then gives uniqueness of the interior optimizer. When
$\E_p[x] \le 0$, $G'(0) \le 0$ and strict concavity implies $G$ is
non-increasing on $[0, 1)$, so $w^\star = 0$ is the optimizer.
\qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{Closed form for \texorpdfstring{$w^\star$}{w-star} in the three-outcome case}

For the three-outcome credit spread setting with payoffs
$\{+1, +\alpha, -1\}$ and probabilities $\{p, r_o, q\}$, the
first-order condition is a quadratic in $w$ admitting the closed-form
solution derived in \citet[Appendix A]{mercurio2026empirical}. We
reproduce the coefficients here for self-containedness:
\begin{equation*}
A = -\alpha, \quad B = \alpha (p - q) - (p + q), \quad C = p + r_o \alpha - q,
\end{equation*}
and $w^\star = (-B - \sqrt{B^2 - 4AC})/(2A)$, where the relevant root
is the unique value in $(0, 1)$.


\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:memm}: MEMM market model}

The body of the paper proves Theorem~\ref{thm:memm} by finite-simplex
KKT (Section~\ref{sec:pricing}). We collect here the underlying
market-model assumptions made implicit in that proof.

\paragraph{Discrete-state market.} The market consists of one risk-free
asset (with gross return normalised to 1 over the single period) and
one risky asset whose period-end payoff is the random variable $X$ on
$\X = \{\omega_1, \ldots, \omega_n\}$. A contingent claim with payoff
$x: \X \to \R$ and cost $c$ is feasible if there exists an equivalent
martingale measure $\Q$ such that $\E_\Q[x] = c$. The set
$\mathcal{Q}$ of such measures is the (non-empty, convex, compact)
polytope $\{ \Q \in \Delta_n : \E_\Q[X] = X_0\}$, where $X_0$ is the
current risky-asset price. The no-arbitrage condition is the
non-emptiness of $\mathcal{Q}$ \citep{follmer2002}.

\paragraph{MEMM existence and uniqueness.} The MEMM is the unique
$\Q^* \in \mathcal{Q}$ minimizing $\KL(\Q \| u_n)$. Existence follows
from continuity of $\Q \mapsto \KL(\Q \| u_n)$ on the compact
$\mathcal{Q}$; uniqueness follows from strict convexity of the KL
functional in $\Q$. The exponential-tilt characterization
\eqref{eq:memm-exp-tilt} is the standard Lagrangian first-order
condition on the simplex.

\paragraph{Convergence of $c^\star_k$.} The interchange of $\lim_{k \to 0}$
and the integral defining $\E_{\Q^*}[x]$ is automatic on the finite
simplex: $c^\star_k$ is a continuous function of $k$ on $[0, \infty)$
(both numerator and denominator of \eqref{eq:reservation-form} are
analytic in $k$), and $|c^\star_k| \le \|x\|_\infty$ is uniformly
bounded. The dominated-convergence step that would be needed in a
continuous-state proof reduces to bounded-convergence on a finite set.

\paragraph{Generalisation to continuous state spaces.} The
discrete-state result above specialises the general result of
\citet[Theorem 2.1]{frittelli2000} and \citet{delbaen2002}, which
require a localising sequence to handle continuous-time martingale
issues. In the discrete setting all of those technicalities collapse:
strict convexity holds pointwise, the simplex is compact, and the
exponential tilt is finite-dimensional.
\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:cts-foc} (continuous-state FOC)}

The continuous-state FOC was derived in Section~\ref{sec:continuous};
the only technical step worth restating is the integrand bound that
licenses the dominated-convergence interchange. With $\|x\|_\infty < 1
/ w_{\max}$ for the maximum admissible $w_{\max} < 1$ in
\eqref{eq:G-canonical}, the integrand
$|p(s) x(s) / (1 + w x(s))|$ is bounded by $p(s) \|x\|_\infty /
(1 - w_{\max} \|x\|_\infty)$, a $p$-integrable function. The
existence of an interior solution follows from continuity and the
boundary behavior $\partial_w G \to +\infty$ as $w \to -1/\|x\|_\infty^+$
and $\partial_w G \to -\infty$ as $w \to 1^-$ under the bounded-payoff
assumption.
